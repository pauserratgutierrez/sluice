## Method changes during the phase

- Derived resume-buffer coverage. Runs whose runner was built before 13:05 UTC derived each probe round's coverage from the change rate of the last sampling interval (5 s), which bursts of messages make noisy: `realistic-main-r3`, `high-main-r1`, `high-main-r2`, `high-main-r3`. From `veryhigh-main-r1` (started 13:17 UTC) the runner averages the last minute live. From `veryhigh-main-r3` on, reports also carry `config.probe_derived_window` (60 s). The phase report recomputes the derived coverage of every run from its timeline with the one-minute window (`RederivedCoverage`) and, for the runs that computed it live, checks that the two agree. The probed coverage (Sluice's own `resumed` answers) is the same method throughout.
  The old single-interval rate was not only noisy but aliased: each room sends a message every 15 s at a fixed phase, so the 5-s rate of `room_members` repeats a 15-s cycle (112, 120, 32 changes/s in `high-main-r3`), and the probe, every 120 s, always read the same point of it (32/s, where the mean over the steady phase is 87.5/s). The one-minute mean (327–333/s in `veryhigh-main-r1`, against 333 expected) does not alias.

## The pprof runs are for diagnosis only

- `sluice:pprof` samples blocking events and contended locks for the whole run (`SLUICE_PPROF_BLOCK_RATE`=100 000 ns, `SLUICE_PPROF_MUTEX_FRACTION`=100), and each `pprof-*` run also takes a 60-s CPU profile and a 5-s execution trace. Those runs say where the CPU goes and whether the reader's goroutine is saturated; they are not used in the performance or capacity tables. Their overhead is estimated by comparing `pprof-veryhigh`'s CPU per 1000 changes with the very high tier's main runs over the same first 10 minutes of the steady phase, with and without the profiling minute and the trace.
- Changes from 17:49 UTC (from `pprof-high` on): the runner splits each message's latency into write → commit (`commit_time`) and commit → client, and reads every second PostgreSQL's WAL end, the walsender's `sent_lsn`/`write_lsn` and Sluice's dispatched position (`/readyz`), turned into seconds behind the WAL end (`stages` and `lsn_timeline` in the report).
- Very high tier, first 10 min of the steady phase, CPU per 1000 changes: r1 367 ms, r2 355 ms, r3 430 ms, all at 337 changes/s. The re-run r3 (16:54–17:25 UTC) overlapped light work on the host (a one-core cross-compile, `go vet` and unit tests of the runner, an image build): process CPU per change is sensitive to what shares the host's cores. The median (367 ms) is the baseline for the pprof overhead; between repetitions, expect ±10%.

## Live memory against garbage (asked 2026-10-09 ~18:45 UTC)

- Sluice 0.8.0's `/metrics` has no `/gc/heap/live:bytes` (client_golang's default Go collector leaves it out), and the timeline recorded heap in use, which includes garbage. Evidence used: (1) the minimum of heap in use per minute, closest to what each GC leaves; (2) from `pprof-unicorn-1cpu` on, `go_memstats_next_gc_bytes` (about twice the live heap with GOGC=100 and GOMEMLIMIT far away) in the timeline; (3) the pprof runs take heap profiles (in-use = live at the last GC) at 2 and 9 min of the steady phase, besides the one after the CPU profile.
- With streams and load constant, the per-minute minimum of heap in use climbs and CPU per change climbs with it, while GCs per minute fall: very high r1 113 → 155 MiB, 360 → 437 ms per 1000 changes; unicorn at 2 CPUs 994 → 1 550 MiB, 505 → 607 ms; unicorn at 1 CPU 1 068 → 1 893 MiB in an hour, 492 → 819 ms. Garbage alone would not raise CPU this way (fewer, larger GC cycles cost about the same per second), so something in the hot path grows.
- Suspect, from the code: `shape.Filter.RoutingKey` picks among equally good columns by map iteration order, and `hold.Index` calls it separately when indexing and unindexing a watch. A hold filter `room_id=eq.R,user_id=eq.U` has two such columns, so about half of `Replace` (every shape on every `/token`) and `Remove` calls leave the old `*Watch` in `byColumn`, alive while the relation has other watches. Every `room_members` UPDATE then evaluates the watches keyed on its room, stale ones included. The registry stores the key it chose and is not affected. Expected stale watches in 30 min: ~10 000 (very high), ~84 000 (unicorn); at 4–7 KB each with their parsed filters, about the growth measured. To confirm after the queue: `internal/hold/hold_leak_test.go` (expected to fail on 0.8.0) and the diff of the 2-min and 9-min heap profiles of `pprof-unicorn-2cpu`.
- For the end of phase 3: a stability run of several hours at the very high tier, to rule out any accumulation once this is fixed.

## Profiles read (2026-10-09 ~20:55–21:30 UTC, no run measuring)

- CPU (60 s at 4 min into steady): `hold.(*Index).OnChange` is 48–52% of Sluice's CPU in every tier (high, very high, unicorn at 1 and 2 CPUs); the reader goroutine as a whole 52–56%; GC workers 19–24%; HTTP handlers 13–18% (SSE writes 9–15%); delivery (`deliver`) ~1.5%. Inside the hold check: `expr.Eval` 37%, `tupleRow.Column` 15% (a `strings.ToLower` per lookup, 5%), `strconv.ParseFloat` failing on text values (6%, its error allocations included).

## UPDATE without an old tuple cuts other rows' holds (Sluice bug, found 2026-10-09 ~21:30 UTC)

- With `REPLICA IDENTITY DEFAULT` (or `USING INDEX`), an UPDATE that leaves the identity columns alone carries no old tuple. `hold.watchBroken` then cuts every candidate watch whose filter the new row does not match. A watch is a candidate when one routed column matches, so with a two-column identity (`room_id, user_id`) the candidates include other rows' holds: updating u2's membership of room R cuts u1's grant held by R (room-first), and with member-first holds, updating u1's membership of R2 cuts u1's grant held by R. Present in 0.8.0 and in the 0.8.1 candidate; FULL is not affected (the old tuple is always there).
- Reproduced in `internal/hold/hold_identity_test.go` (fails on the unfixed code: four cases, three false cuts and one extra). Fix prepared in the 0.8.1 worktree, pending the user's decision: without an old tuple, the old row's identity columns are the new row's and its other columns unknown; cut only if that old row may have matched.

## Findings for the guide (rule — why — figure — where measured)

Figures are Sluice 0.8.0 unless noted; to be re-measured on the latest version before the guide is written.

1. ~~Prefer the primary key's replica identity~~ **Withdrawn (2026-10-09 ~21:30 UTC): the primary-key runs are invalid.** Their low CPU (74 and 71 ms per 1 000 changes) came from Sluice cutting nearly every grant, not from cheaper decoding: `veryhigh-pk` delivered 301 events/s of 502 expected, forgot all 10 500 subscriptions and counted 1.14 M `shape_not_authorized` errors (each a hold cut). Cause: a Sluice bug, below ("UPDATE without an old tuple"). FULL against the primary key is to be re-measured once that is fixed. What remains true: with FULL, every candidate hold is evaluated twice per UPDATE (old and new row), with the primary key once.
2. **The cost follows the WAL's changes, not the events delivered.** Each message writes 1 + N rows (an UPDATE of `last_message_at` per member); most reach no stream, and all are decoded and routed. — calibrate: 787 changes/s to deliver 1 325 events/s; dispatch averaged 167 µs per change. — `calibrate-laptop` (phase 1). Alternatives to measure in phase 4: one row on the parent (the room) instead of one per member, computing "last message" at read time, a channel.
3. **Ephemeral state (typing) belongs on channels: it skips the WAL entirely.** — typing p95 1–2 ms at 100–1 500 streams and 15–25 ms at 12 000 streams on 2 CPUs, while WAL-borne messages waited 0.3–0.6 s behind the reader. — `veryhigh-main-r1`, `unicorn-2cpu`.
4. **`synchronous_commit=off` delays every change by up to ~3 × `wal_writer_delay`.** The walsender only sends flushed WAL. — message p50 177 ms (off) against 3 ms (on). — `rooms-smoke`, `rooms-smoke-synccommit` (phase 1).
5. **One slot's changes/s ceiling is one thread's: Sluice decodes and dispatches on the reader goroutine.** More CPUs help only as far as that goroutine gets a whole core. — unicorn, 2 320 changes/s: 1 CPU dispatched ~1 950/s and fell behind for good; 2 CPUs kept up (1.29 cores in use) with the reader ~0.09 s (p50) and ~0.45 s (p95) behind the WAL end, and that delay was the whole of commit → client (p95 0.44 s). — `unicorn-main-r1`, `unicorn-2cpu` (clean run), `stages`. Confirmed by the traces: at 1 CPU the reader goroutine runs 93% of the time and never waits for WAL; at 2 CPUs it runs 50% and its lag comes from busy spans of up to ~0.9 s in hold checks (`pprof-unicorn-*`). In 0.8.0 the single thread's work is mostly hold checks, so the ceiling per slot is set by how many holds each change must evaluate.
6. **Big transactions do not stall other streams; they cost their own dispatch time.** — 100 000 rows: commit ~350 ms, walsender sent ~400 ms after commit, Sluice dispatched ~490 ms after; backlog peak 8–26 MiB; other streams' message p95 14–17 ms before and during. — `bigtxn-high`.
7. **Memory per stream (7 shapes + a hook channel, issuer mode): ~80–90 KiB of live heap, ~93 KiB of Go memory.** — 15 000 idle streams in 1.4 GiB of Go memory at 0.13 cores (hook re-checks and token renewals). — `idle-ramp`. Inflated by finding 9; to re-measure on 0.8.1. A 512 MB container (GOMEMLIMIT ~460 MiB) fits at most ~4 900 such streams, not the ~8 000 of the README (which measured 5 shapes and a public channel in RLS mode).
8. **The resume buffer covers a table for `SLUICE_RING_EVENTS` of its changes, whoever they were for.** A table that takes N rows per action exhausts it fastest. — `room_members`: ~45–60 s at 89 changes/s, ~10 s at 337/s, ~2 s at ~1 950/s; `room_messages` always ~60 s (`SLUICE_RING_MAX_AGE`). — tiers' probes.
9. **Hold filters: put the most selective column first.** (0.8.1 routes by the first equally good column; 0.8.0 picked one at random and leaked watches.) — 0.8.1, first 10 min: very high 95 ms of CPU per 1 000 changes with `user_id` first against 365 with `room_id` first (0.03 against 0.12 cores); unicorn at 2 CPUs 105 ms with `user_id` first against 535 on 0.8.0, message p95 6 ms against 305. — `v081-*` (`../phase3/v0.8.1.md`).
10. **Per-stream background cost: every `/token` re-asks the issuer once per shape, one after another, and every hook re-check (ttl 0) costs a call per stream per catalog tick.** — `/token` p50 ~92–130 ms with 7 shapes at 5–20 ms per issuer call; reconnect storms: 8 issuer calls per stream (7 shapes + hook). — tiers, phase 1 reconnect smoke runs.
